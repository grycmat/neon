Neon

Last updated: July 28, 2026

This document covers the Neon Android app (package com.gigapingu.neon): what it does with your data, and its standards for user conduct and content — including its Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) policy.

1. What Neon is

Neon is a client app for Mastodon, a federated social network made up of many independently operated servers ("instances"). Neon itself does not host, host accounts on, or moderate any of the posts, profiles, or media you see — that content lives on and is moderated by whichever Mastodon instance you sign in to. Neon is a window into your existing (or newly created) account on that instance, similar in role to a web browser or an email client.

2. Information Neon collects and stores

  • Your OAuth access token, obtained when you sign in to your chosen instance, is stored on your device (Android DataStore) so you stay signed in. It is sent only to your instance's own API — never to the developer or any third party.
  • A local cache of posts, profiles, and other timeline data you've already loaded, stored on-device (Room database) purely to make the app feel faster and work offline. This cache never leaves your device.
  • Push notifications (optional, off by default): if enabled, your device generates a private key pair and registers a Web Push subscription with your instance, routed through a self-hosted relay (mastodon-fcm-relay) and delivered via Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google). The relay and Firebase only ever see an encrypted payload and your FCM device token — the private key needed to decrypt notification content never leaves your device, so neither the relay nor Firebase can read your notification content.
  • Turning off push notifications or logging out removes the push subscription and erases the on-device key pair.

3. What Neon does not do

  • No advertising, ad IDs, or ad networks.
  • No third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • No separate "Neon account" or developer-run database of your posts, contacts, or profile — your account and its data belong to your chosen Mastodon instance and are governed by that instance's own privacy policy and rules.

4. Third parties involved

  • Your Mastodon instance — hosts your account and content; see its own privacy policy and rules.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) — transport for push notifications (encrypted payload + device token only).
  • The self-hosted mastodon-fcm-relay — forwards your still-encrypted push payload from your instance to Firebase; operated by the developer, never sees plaintext notification content.

5. Data deletion

Logging out clears your locally stored token and push key pair and removes your push subscription. Uninstalling the app removes all local data (cache, token, keys) from your device.

6. Children's privacy

Neon is not directed at children and is not knowingly used to collect data from anyone under the minimum age required by their chosen Mastodon instance's own terms (Mastodon instances generally require users to be at least 13, and this app requires an existing or newly created account on such an instance to be used at all). If you believe a child has provided personal information through this app, contact us at the address in section 8 and it will be removed from any developer-controlled system (see section 2 — in practice, the developer holds none of this data; it resides on your chosen instance).

7. Community Standards — Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE)

Neon strictly prohibits Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) and Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) in any form, on any instance accessed through this app. This is a zero-tolerance standard.

Because Neon is a client application, the posts and profiles you see are hosted and primarily moderated by the Mastodon instance you've connected to, under that instance's own rules and moderation team — not by the Neon developer.

In-app reporting and blocking. Every post and every profile in Neon can be reported directly from the app (long-press a post, or the overflow menu on a profile) — this submits a report to your instance's moderation team, with an optional comment, and takes effect immediately. You can also block or mute any account directly from its profile at any time, which stops its posts from appearing to you.

Our response to CSAE/CSAM. If the developer obtains actual knowledge of CSAE or CSAM being displayed through the app:

  1. The offending account/content will be blocked so it can no longer be rendered within Neon.
  2. It will be reported to the administrators of the hosting instance for removal and action under their own policies.
  3. Where required by law, it will be reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) or the equivalent authority for the relevant jurisdiction.
